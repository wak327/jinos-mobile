# Cairn: a clean-slate mainline + Rust phone OS

Design proposal, track "purist mainline + Rust". Date: 2026-10-02. All status claims were checked against 2025-2026 sources (URLs inline); items I could not verify are marked **unverified**.

## 0. Thesis in one paragraph

Cairn is an immutable, image-updated Linux phone OS that runs only the upstream kernel, reaches hardware only through mainline drivers plus redistributable firmware, and puts everything new in Rust: a Smithay compositor, an Iced shell, a Varlink permission broker, a power manager and a portal backend. It does not reuse Android drivers, libhybris, Halium or hwcomposer. It accepts that in 2026 this means two Fairphones and a dev board, and argues that this is the right trade: the mainline enablement of 2024-2026 phones is now happening at vendor speed (Fairphone posts patches on launch day, Qualcomm/Linaro post SoC support before announcement), and the only way to benefit from it is to stand on top of it rather than beside it. The user-visible differentiator is a "glass box" phone: every radio, sensor, camera, microphone, network and wakeup event is attributed to the process that caused it, shown to the user, and cuttable at the compositor or portal, which an Android phone cannot do for its own vendor and Google processes.

## 1. Hardware adaptation

### 1.1 The line: firmware yes, drivers no

Cairn uses mainline Linux drivers for every subsystem and loads vendor firmware blobs into the remote processors (modem, ADSP, CDSP, GPU zap shader, Wi-Fi/BT, video) exactly the way Nura (ex-postmarketOS) does: upstream `linux-firmware` first, packaged `soc-qcom-*-nonfree-firmware` second, and per-unit calibration (modem NV, Wi-Fi board data, camera OTP) pulled from the phone's own partitions at boot with `msm-firmware-loader` ([firmware policy](https://docs.nura.eco/pmaports/main/packaging/firmware-packages.html)). No bionic code runs on the CPU. There is no Android container, no libhybris, no hwcomposer, no Treble/GSI, and therefore no "Android base version"; the only Android artefact in the system is the optional Waydroid image (section 6).

Why not "both" (mainline where available, Halium where not)? Because there is no Rust or wlroots-0.20 compositor with a hwcomposer backend (Droidian's wlroots fork is 2-3 major versions behind, KWin dropped Halium, Mir's backend is Qt5-bound), so "both" means one developer writing and owning a libhybris backend for Smithay before writing anything else ([compositor table in the modern-stack report](https://github.com/droidian/wlroots/branches)). It also forfeits the features that justify the track: Rust kernel drivers, Landlock (absent on android-5.10 kernels), `sched_ext`, modern BPF, fscrypt v2 defaults, and a kernel that receives security fixes after the OEM leaves ([Landlock ABI history](https://docs.kernel.org/userspace-api/landlock.html)).

### 1.3 Deliberate SoC and device selection, and the 24-month argument

The research is unambiguous that "SoC upstream" is not "phone upstream": SM8650 is fully featured upstream, yet no SM8450-SM8750 phone is at community tier. So Cairn chooses hardware by three criteria, in order: (a) the vendor itself posts kernel patches, (b) bootloader unlock is a documented, quota-free procedure with kernel sources published, (c) the SoC family already has a Nura generic SoC package and U-Boot support.

How this reaches "modern phones within 24 months" concretely:

1. **Fairphone's cadence is now measurable.** FP4/FP5 went from "mostly broken" (FOSDEM 2024) to "daily driver, depending on definition" (FOSDEM 2026). FP6 reached cameras, audio and an IMS emergency call at month 14 after launch, faster than FP5 did. Riding that curve, FP6 is the modern phone that becomes daily-driver grade inside the window, and Fairphone's Software Longevity Team is the de-facto partner (we send test reports, UCM files, libcamera tuning and panel fixes upstream; they review SoC patches). A Murena-style preinstall partnership is the five-year goal, not the two-year one.
2. **Qualcomm/Linaro remove the SoC half of the problem for flagships.** SM8650/8750/8850 and unannounced SoCs are posted upstream on day one, with U-Boot and EFI; Qualcomm's own BSP moved to "upstream-first" in June 2026. What remains per phone is panel, touch, charger, audio routing, camera sensors, haptics, NFC: roughly a developer-year on a phone whose kernel sources and unlock exist. That is why it is a Tier 2 stretch behind a contributor, not the primary plan.
3. **Choosing one SoC family twice.** QCM6490 covers FP5, SHIFTphone 8 and Dragon Q6A with one generic kernel and one device-quirks database; SM7635 is the same DPU/CAMSS/ADSP lineage one generation on. Two SoC families, four devices, one audio and camera stack.
4. **Partnering where partners already exist.** Nura (device packages, generic kernels, hardware CI PCB), Linaro (U-Boot, SM8xxx), Fairphone and SHIFT (patches, unlock), Radxa/Qualcomm (Dragon Q6A, Qualcomm Linux 2.0), NGI0/NLnet (openimsd, q6voice, Mobile NixOS on QCS6490 were all funded this way: [NLnet](https://nlnet.nl/project/MobileNixOS-SDM845-QCS6490/)).

## 2. Boot, kernel, init, updates

**Boot chain.** ABL -> `boot_a/boot_b` containing U-Boot packaged as an Android boot image -> U-Boot UEFI -> `systemd-boot` -> signed UKI (kernel + initrd + cmdline + dm-verity root hash + DTBs). The UKI carries all supported device trees as `.dtbauto` sections so one image boots every Cairn device (systemd-stub v256+ picks the DTB by compatible string; **verify behaviour against U-Boot's EFI implementation per device**). This is the boot flow Nura's "main" category requires and Duranium already uses ([Duranium](https://postmarketos.org/blog/2026/03/17/introducing-duranium/), [PMCR-0009](https://docs.nura.eco/pmcr/main/0009-new-main-device-category.html)).

**Storage without repartitioning.** The stock Android GPT stays. Cairn lives inside `userdata`, exposed to U-Boot as a block device via `blkmap` with its own GPT: ESP (512 MB), two 4 GB EROFS `/usr` slots, a LUKS2 `/var`, and `/home` managed by `systemd-homed`. Factory reset = discard keys and re-run `systemd-repart`. Borrowed wholesale from Duranium.

**Kernel.** One generic aarch64 kernel per release, tracking the newest kernel.org stable (7.1.y at the time of writing) with an LTS fallback, built with LLVM, `CONFIG_RUST=y`, kconfig assembled from fragments and checked by a `kconfigcheck.toml`-style invariant file (firewall, Landlock, fscrypt, cgroup v2 freezer, binderfs for Waydroid, BPF). Rule copied from Nura verbatim: out-of-tree or non-upstreamable patches are unacceptable in the generic kernel; the only tolerated exception is a time-boxed `q6voice` patch while it is being upstreamed ([generic kernels](https://docs.nura.eco/pmaports/main/packaging/kernel-packages/generic-kernel-packages.html)). Rust in the kernel is no longer experimental (Dec 2025), so Cairn can write new drivers (haptics, fuel gauges, panel quirks) in Rust where maintainers accept it ([LKML](https://lkml.iu.edu/2512.1/04944.html)).

**Init.** systemd 261/262 (262 added a persistent installed-files DB and a Varlink API to `sysupdate`, sysext merge from the initrd, and fscrypt v2 defaults in homed; [NEWS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/NEWS)). No OpenRC option.

**Immutable root and updates.** `/usr` is EROFS under dm-verity with the root hash in the UKI; `/etc` is factory-default symlinks plus a writable overlay; `/var` and `/home` encrypted. OTA is `systemd-sysupdate` pulling `SHA256SUMS`-signed UKIs and `/usr` images from a static HTTPS server (channels = directories), installing into the inactive slot, and `systemd-boot` boot counting plus `systemd-bless-boot` marking a slot good only after the shell reaches the lock screen and the modem registers. Rollback is automatic after three failed boots (Duranium validated the fallback-entry path on U-Boot EFI; **EFI runtime variable access must be verified per device**). Optional layers (Waydroid, debug tools, vendor firmware packs, display-out drivers) ship as `systemd-sysext` images with the same signing, which is how Duranium ships Waydroid (`updatectl enable waydroid`, [Nura June 2026](https://nura.eco/blog/2026/07/06/pmOS-update-2026-06/)). Deltas are deferred; RAUC's streaming bundles are the fallback if sysupdate's delta story stays thin ([RAUC](https://rauc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/changes.html)).

**Verified boot.** Honest limits: on an unlocked bootloader (ORANGE) the chain is unverified until U-Boot. From U-Boot onward Cairn enables U-Boot UEFI Secure Boot with its own `db` key, so UKIs are verified, and dm-verity protects `/usr`. Where an OEM supports a user-settable root of trust (`avb_custom_key`), Cairn signs the U-Boot `boot.img` with its AVB key for YELLOW state; FP5 relock with custom keys has brick reports and is not recommended ([iodé community](https://community.iode.tech/t/close-the-bootloader-back-on-fp5/8135), [Fairphone](https://support.fairphone.com/hc/en-us/articles/10492476238865)). Published key fingerprints (`attestation.json` style) from day one, borrowed from GrapheneOS ([guide](https://grapheneos.org/articles/attestation-compatibility-guide)).

## 3. Base system

**Decision: Alpine/Nura as the package substrate, apk, musl, systemd; Cairn is an overlay repository plus its own images and channels.** Nura is where mainline phone enablement velocity lives: generic kernels, `device-fairphone-fp5/fp6`, `qcom-qcs6490` SoC packages, firmware packaging, `msm-firmware-loader`, `hexagonrpcd`, `q6voiced`, `imsd`, `callaudiod`, `feedbackd`, hardware CI and the Duranium image tooling ([v26.06](https://nura.eco/blog/2026/06/21/v26.06-release/), [Alpine 3.24: Rust 1.96, LLVM 22](https://alpinelinux.org/posts/Alpine-3.24.0-released.html)). Re-creating that on Debian or Fedora would cost the one developer the year needed for the Rust layer. Nura already carries the systemd-on-musl patches and ships systemd 261; musl is a non-issue for Rust (first-class target) and for Flatpak apps (they bundle their runtime). Cairn's overlay `aports` holds the Rust components, the shell, the portal backend and the image definitions; device and kernel packages are consumed from pmaports unchanged, and fixes go upstream there. Note Nura's contribution policy forbids generative-AI-assisted contributions ([Feb 2026 update](https://nura.eco/blog/2026/02/26/pmOS-update-2026-02/)); Cairn's own repos set their own policy, and every patch headed upstream must comply with the target project's rules.

**Build.** `pmbootstrap` 3.10 (Python) builds packages and the Duranium-style images today; `cairnctl` (host tool, Go is acceptable here because it is off-device and the developer is fastest in it; everything on the device is Rust) wraps it, validates `device.toml`, assembles UKIs, signs, and publishes channels. Rust components are a single Cargo workspace built with the Alpine toolchain in CI and vendored (`cargo vendor`) for reproducibility.

**Fallback if the Nura dependency becomes a blocker:** `mkosi` with Fedora packages (Fedora Mobility SIG, fresh Mesa/libcamera/systemd, glibc) producing the same EROFS+UKI+sysupdate layout; the switching cost is re-packaging the ~40 device/firmware/telephony packages, roughly three developer-months, and is the reason to keep Cairn's on-device code distro-agnostic (no apk calls at runtime, no Alpine-specific paths).

## 4. Graphics and compositor

**`cairn-comp`: a Smithay 0.7 compositor (MIT, Rust), separate from the shell process.** Smithay provides DRM/KMS with hardware-plane assignment (`DrmCompositor`), libinput, libseat sessions, GLES rendering, and the protocols a phone needs: `wlr-layer-shell`, `xdg-activation`, `ext-session-lock`, `input-method-v2`/`text-input-v3`, screencopy, `security-context`, fractional scale, presentation-time, and the winit backend that runs the same compositor nested on a desktop ([Smithay](https://github.com/Smithay/smithay), [crates.io 0.7.0, 2025-06-24](https://crates.io/crates/smithay)). cosmic-comp is the structural reference (COSMIC 1.8 added touch input, 1.9 shipped `cosmic-osk` on 2026-09-24, [linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/?p=219883)), and Catacomb (GPL-3, moved to SourceHut in May 2026) is the mobile-gesture reference to read, not fork ([catacomb](https://github.com/catacombing/catacomb)).

Composition offload borrows the HWC idea on KMS: fullscreen apps and the shell's layer surfaces are assigned to DPU planes with explicit fences and VSYNC latching; GPU composition only for the remainder. GPU drivers are Mesa 26.x Freedreno/Turnip; the renderer is GLES today with Zink as the GL path on Vulkan-only GPUs. No hwcomposer backend will ever be written. The compositor is privileged and minimal; the shell is an unprivileged layer-shell client, so a shell crash does not kill apps (the phoc/Phosh model).

## 5. Shell and UI toolkit, and the differentiator

**Toolkit decision: Iced 0.14 via libcosmic (MIT)** for `cairn-shell`, `cairn-osk` (fork of cosmic-osk), `cairn-greeter` (fork of cosmic-greeter) and Settings ([Iced 0.14.0, 2025-12-07](https://crates.io/crates/iced)). Reasons: it is the only Rust toolkit shipping inside a Smithay compositor to real users with touch and an OSK; layer-shell integration exists; MIT matches Smithay. Runner-up Slint 1.18 (GPLv3 or royalty-free; its royalty-free terms cover "mobile applications" but define an "embedded system" as a device for a specific task, and say nothing about OS shells, [Slint pricing](https://slint.dev/pricing)); Cairn would need written confirmation or accept GPLv3 for the shell, so Slint is offered as the app-developer DSL, not the shell toolkit. Xilem/Masonry is alpha. Qt6/QML is rejected for the shell: Qt Wayland Compositor is GPLv3-or-commercial, the stack is C++, and nobody has shipped a Qt6 phone shell ([Qt licence](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qtwaylandcompositor-index.html)). GTK4/libadwaita remains first-class for apps.

**Shell architecture.** Lock screen, home, app grid, card switcher, quick settings and notification shade are one process; widgets ("tiles") are separate sandboxed layer-shell clients launched by the system on a schedule (OpenHarmony's `FormExtensionAbility` pattern: the system owns the lifecycle, [stage model](https://gitcode.com/openharmony/docs/blob/master/en/application-dev/application-models/stage-model-development-overview.md)). Gesture navigation (edge swipes, pinch to overview) from Catacomb; notch and rounded-corner data from `gmobile`-style device quirks.

**Differentiator: the glass box.** Android's privacy dashboard excludes vendor and Google processes; Cairn attributes everything. `cairn-powerd` owns `/sys/power/wakeup_count` and wakelocks, `cairn-permd` brokers every camera, microphone, location, contacts, telephony and network grant, the compositor draws indicators it alone controls, and an eBPF (Aya) tracer attributes wakeups, network bytes and radio-on time to cgroups. The user gets a timeline ("your modem was awake 42 minutes last night: 31 for app X") and kill switches per app and per device class that are enforced below the app. Nothing here requires new hardware; it requires the whole system being one design, which is exactly what a clean slate buys. Convergence (external display, desktop mode) is a Phase 3 feature, deliberately not the headline (every dead project led with it).

## 6. App model

- **Native:** Rust (Iced or Slint) and GTK4/libadwaita, both via Flatpak. Qt6 apps run from the KDE runtime. A `cairn-sdk` Flatpak extension provides Iced templates and `flatpak-cargo-generator` manifests.
- **Web/PWA:** installed PWAs run in a WebKitGTK-based runner (GNOME Web is the reference; Tauri's Verso runtime died in Oct 2025, [Tauri blog](https://v2.tauri.app/blog/tauri-verso-integration/)). No web-first ecosystem bet (Firefox OS lesson).
- **Flutter:** supported for apps through `flutter-elinux`'s Wayland embedder inside Flatpak; never for the shell ([sony/flutter-embedded-linux](https://github.com/sony/flutter-embedded-linux)).
- **Android:** Waydroid 1.6.3 (Android 13 LineageOS image, initial Android 16) as an opt-in sysext using mainline binder; stated up front that Play Integrity will fail, so banking/government apps may not work ([Waydroid](https://github.com/waydroid/waydroid/tags), [Play Integrity](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/improvements)).
- **Packaging:** Flatpak 1.18 for third-party apps, Flathub as the catalogue with a mobile-adaptive filter; system components as sysext images; no snap (Canonical-centric, AppArmor-dependent, absent from every mobile distro), no click (Qt5 world), OCI only as a transport if Flathub's OCI remotes are ever needed ([Flatpak 1.18.4](https://github.com/flatpak/flatpak/releases/tag/1.18.4)).
- **Sandbox and permissions:** bubblewrap + seccomp from Flatpak, Landlock rules added by the launcher on top, `security-context` tagging in the compositor so sandboxed clients cannot screencopy or grab global shortcuts, per-app cgroup for freezing and power accounting, per-app network via cgroup-matched nftables rules. Portals are the only route to user data: `xdg-desktop-portal` 1.22 with `xdg-desktop-portal-cairn` (Rust) as backend, implementing FileChooser, Camera (PipeWire), Location, ScreenCast, Notification, Background, Inhibit, Settings and Access, plus Cairn-specific portals for telephony and SMS so dialers and messengers never touch ModemManager directly ([xdg-desktop-portal](https://github.com/flatpak/xdg-desktop-portal/releases)). Permission semantics copy Android 15-17 and OpenHarmony: one-time, session-scoped and persistent grants, picker-first access, a stable app identity carried on every connection (OpenHarmony's TokenID idea, [AccessToken](https://gitcode.com/openharmony/security_access_token/blob/master/README.md)).

## 7. Telephony, audio, camera, sensors, power

**Telephony.** ModemManager 1.24 over QRTR/QMI with low-power mode during suspend and Cell Broadcast; `imsd` for VoLTE/SMS-over-IMS with `openimsd` tracked as the alternative; `lpac` for eSIM (SGP.22) where the modem exposes APDUs; `mobile-broadband-provider-info` for emergency numbers; a Rust `cairn-telephonyd` holds call/SMS state over `zbus` and exposes the telephony portal ([ModemManager 1.24 NEWS](https://sources.debian.org/src/modemmanager/1.24.2-2/NEWS), [lpac](https://github.com/estkme-group/lpac)). RCS is treated as unavailable (Matrix/Signal plus SMS/MMS).

**Audio.** PipeWire 1.6 + WirePlumber, ALSA UCM2 profiles per device, `callaudiod` for call routing, `q6voiced` for the DSP voice path on QCM6490/SM7635-class parts. The known hole is voice-call audio on AudioReach SoCs (SM8450 and newer); Cairn budgets this as part of any Tier 2 port and co-funds the upstreaming of q6voice.

**Camera.** `qcom-camss` + libcamera 0.7.x SoftISP on the GPU (OpenGL today, Vulkan compute planned) through PipeWire's libcamera SPA, so any portal-aware app sees the cameras; a Rust camera app on `libcamera-rs`; per-sensor tuning files live in the device package. Expectation set publicly at "basic capture, improving", and the Qualcomm OPE driver is watched as the first hardware-ISP template.

**Sensors.** `hexagonrpcd` self-gated on `/dev/fastrpc-adsp`, `iio-sensor-proxy` for orientation and ambient light, `feedbackd` for haptics and LEDs.

**Power.** `cairn-powerd` implements Android's `SystemSuspend` and Doze patterns on upstream primitives: opportunistic autosleep gated by `wakeup_count`, wakelock handles over Varlink with per-cgroup budgets, cgroup v2 freezer for background apps with maintenance windows, exactly one sanctioned wake path (a built-in UnifiedPush distributor), ModemManager low-power during suspend, and eBPF attribution feeding the glass-box timeline ([Android power](https://source.android.com/docs/core/power/mgmt), [Doze](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)). Target: 48 h standby on FP5 by Phase 2; honest baseline is that no mainline phone has demonstrated Android-parity standby yet.

## 8. Security and privacy model

Memory-safe system layer (Android's 2025 data: Rust code at roughly 1000x lower memory-safety vulnerability density than C++, [Google](https://blog.google/security/rust-in-android-move-fast-fix-things/)); every Cairn daemon is a hardened systemd unit (`DynamicUser`, `ProtectSystem=strict`, `RestrictAddressFamilies`, seccomp filters) speaking Varlink via `zlink` (same author as zbus; [zlink](https://docs.rs/zlink), [talk](https://media.ccc.de/v/all-systems-go-2025-340-forget-zbus-zlink-is-the-future-of-ipc-in-rust)). Verified boot as in section 2; dm-verity `/usr`; LUKS2 `/var`; `systemd-homed` with fscrypt v2 homes and Argon2 passphrases (no TPM on phones; TEE-bound keys are future work); default-deny inbound nftables; no root shell unless Developer Mode is enabled on-device (then SSH over USB with key auth); `sudo-rs`; USB role switching via the Rust `usb-signaller`. Privacy: no telemetry, portals only, session-scoped grants, compositor-owned indicators, cgroup-level kill switches, and a reproducible-build and SBOM pipeline from day one because the Cyber Resilience Act's reporting duties started 11 Sep 2026 and any partner preinstalling Cairn becomes a manufacturer ([CRA timeline](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/cyber-resilience-act-implementation)). Threat model stated openly: physical attacker with an unlocked bootloader can replace U-Boot; the mitigation is encryption at rest plus published key fingerprints, not pretending to have GREEN boot.

## 9. Developer experience and build infrastructure

**A device port is a directory,** `devices/<vendor>-<codename>/`, containing `device.toml` (validated and documented by `cairnctl lint`, the `deviceinfo` + `dint` idea), the DTB name(s) and kconfig fragments (no kernel fork), a firmware manifest (which blobs come from `linux-firmware`, which are packaged, which are extracted from partitions), UCM2 profiles, libcamera tuning, `gmobile`-style quirks (notch, corners, orientation), and a hardware test list consumed by CI. Device tiers with teeth, copied from Nura: Bench (boots in CI), Daily (calls, SMS, data, Wi-Fi, audio, charging, camera, 24 h standby, auto-upgrade test passes), Reference (hardware CI in two locations). Devices are demoted automatically when their tests fail for a release.

**CI.** Forge-hosted (GitLab or GitHub) with self-hosted aarch64 runners; every merge builds the x86_64 and aarch64 images, boots the aarch64 image in QEMU `virt` with virtio-gpu and runs the shell's integration tests headless (Smithay's headless backend), and runs the hardware-in-the-loop suite on FP5 and Dragon Q6A via CI-tron and the open Nura phone-harness PCB (reboot, kernel upgrade, call, suspend-drain) ([hardware CI docs](https://docs.nura.eco/hardware-ci)).

**Shell on a desktop.** `cairn-comp --nested` (winit backend) runs inside any Wayland session at phone geometry with touch emulation; shell, OSK, portals and Flatpak apps run unchanged. This is how contributors without a Fairphone work, and the x86_64 QEMU image is the "emulator" (Oniro's lesson: emulator and IDE before hardware).

## 11. Roadmap (1-3 people) and the proving milestone

Months are elapsed time for one full-time developer; with three people the shell and hardware streams run in parallel and the dates compress by roughly 40 percent.

- **Phase 0, months 0-2: substrate.** Cairn overlay repo, FP5 image with the Duranium layout built by `cairnctl`, `cairn-comp` minimal (DRM on FP5, nested on desktop), CI with QEMU boots. Dragon Q6A on the bench.
- **Milestone M1, month 5: "Calls on Cairn".** FP5 boots a signed UKI through U-Boot/EFI into an EROFS+dm-verity A/B image; the Rust compositor and a minimal Iced shell (lock, launcher, OSK) run on Freedreno; the phone places and receives a call and SMS, uses mobile data and Wi-Fi, takes an OTA and rolls back automatically after a deliberately broken update; the identical shell runs nested on a desktop. This proves the thesis (mainline + Rust + immutable can be a phone on a vendor-supported current-generation device) before any announcement.
- **Phase 1, months 5-10: shell MVP and permissions.** Notifications, quick settings, card switcher, Settings, `cairn-permd` + `xdg-desktop-portal-cairn`, Flatpak/Flathub, `cairn-powerd` with freezer and wakelock accounting, glass-box timeline v1, FP5 hardware CI in one location.
- **Phase 2, months 10-16: the modern phone.** Fairphone (Gen. 6) tracking Nura edge: Gen 8 Turnip, audio UCM, SoftISP tuning, imsd VoLTE, lpac eSIM; camera app; 48 h standby target on FP5; second CI location; SHIFTphone 8 as a sibling port. First public "daily driver beta" on FP5, FP6 "testing".
- **Phase 3, months 16-24: breadth and hardening.** Waydroid sysext, U-Boot Secure Boot chain, systemd-homed migration, one Tier 2 flagship port if a contributor owns the device, external display, 1.0 with two Daily devices and a six-month release cadence.

## 12. Top risks and mitigations

1. **Per-device enablement stalls (audio, camera, sensors, fingerprint).** Mitigation: Fairphone-first, two SoC families only, co-fund upstream work, publish tiers honestly.
2. **VoLTE stays experimental while 2G/3G shut down.** Mitigation: ship imsd with carrier test matrix, contribute to openimsd, state market limitations on the download page.
3. **No FOSS voice-call audio on AudioReach SoCs** blocks every SM8450+ flagship. Mitigation: treat flagships as Tier 2 until q6voice-for-AudioReach exists; fund it.
4. **Standby battery life never reaches parity.** Mitigation: `cairn-powerd` and eBPF attribution from Phase 1, suspend-drain as a CI metric, modem low-power mode.
5. **Smithay/Iced maturity and small maintainer base** (Smithay 0.7 is from June 2025; Catacomb went dormant on GitHub). Mitigation: vendor crates, contribute fixes upstream, keep the compositor small, reuse cosmic-comp/cosmic-osk code.
6. **Adreno Gen 8 driver stays experimental** (FP6, all 8 Elite phones). Mitigation: FP5 (A643) as reference until Mesa stabilises; track whitebelyash/Mesa; Zink fallback.
7. **Bootloader lockdown spreads** (OnePlus, Xiaomi, Samsung already closed). Mitigation: Fairphone/SHIFT/Radxa focus; watch Motorola 2027.
8. **Single-developer bus factor and burnout.** Mitigation: emulator-first contributor path, non-profit entity by month 12, written policies (AI, CoC) before contributors arrive, pick a trademarkable name early.
9. **No Play Integrity means no banking apps.** Mitigation: say so; web banking and Waydroid as fallbacks; attestation advocacy, never spoofing.
10. **Dependency on Nura and its policies (musl, AI contribution ban).** Mitigation: distro-agnostic on-device code; mkosi/Fedora fallback costed at about three months.
11. **Camera quality perceived as 2018-grade.** Mitigation: set expectations publicly, invest in libcamera tuning, follow the OPE hardware-ISP work.
12. **Fairphone as the only vendor partner.** Mitigation: SHIFT and Radxa as second and third, Qualcomm Linux 2.0 for QCS6490 as a vendor-backed BSP.
